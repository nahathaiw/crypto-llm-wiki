# Why crypto? Trust, middlemen and double-spending

## In one sentence
Crypto tries to let two people pay each other online without a bank in the middle, by replacing trust in a company with cryptographic proof [S1][S5].

## Key ideas
- Today, online payments go through a trusted intermediary such as a bank or card network [S1][S5].
- Because these payments can be reversed, the middleman has to handle disputes. This raises costs and makes very small payments impractical [S1].
- Digital money has a special problem called **double-spending**: a file can be copied, so how do you stop someone from spending the same coin twice? [S1]
- A central "mint" could check for double-spends, but then the whole system depends on that one company [S1].
- Bitcoin's answer is to announce every transaction publicly and have the network agree on one order of transactions, where the earliest one counts [S1].
- The goal, in the whitepaper's words, is "an electronic payment system based on cryptographic proof instead of trust" [S1].
- Bitcoin started in 2009 with the aim of peer-to-peer, censorship-resistant money [S5][S7].
- People use crypto for peer-to-peer value transfer and for smart contracts, for example lending, trading and digital collectibles [S5].
