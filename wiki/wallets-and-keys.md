# Wallets, public keys and private keys

## In one sentence
A crypto wallet holds two keys: a public key that others use to send you crypto, and a private key that proves you own it and lets you spend it [S8].

## Key ideas
- **Public key:** used to receive crypto. "Think of your public key as your mailing address." Sharing it does not let anyone take your money [S8].
- **Private key:** "functions as a password to your crypto wallet and should be kept secret." Anyone who gets it can take all the crypto in that wallet [S8].
- To spend coins, the owner **signs** the transaction with the private key, and the network can't validate the transaction without that signature [S1][S8].
- A Bitcoin coin is "a chain of digital signatures": each payment is signed over to the next owner's public key [S1].
- Many wallets hide the private key behind a **seed phrase** (secret recovery phrase), a string of random words [S8].
- **Custodial vs self-custody:** exchange wallets such as Coinbase or Binance hold your private key and control the funds on your behalf [S8].
- A lost or stolen private key can't be recovered [S8], and with no central authority there is limited recourse [S5].
- Safety: write the seed phrase down, keep copies in more than one place, and never screenshot or photograph it [S8].

## Beginner analogy (AI-written, not from a source)
The public key is your mailbox address, which anyone can drop letters in. The private key is the only key that opens the mailbox, and there's no locksmith to call if you lose it.
