# Stablecoins

## In one sentence
Stablecoins are crypto tokens designed to stay at a fixed value, usually $1, so you get crypto's speed without its price swings [S3][S5].

## Key ideas
- They exist because normal crypto prices are volatile, which makes them awkward for everyday payments [S3].
- **Fiat-backed** (USDT, USDC): each token is backed 1:1 by dollars held by an issuer. Pro: very stable. Con: centralized, and you need audits to trust the reserves [S3][S5].
- **Crypto-backed** (DAI/USDS): backed by more crypto than it is worth, for example at least $2 of crypto for each $1 coin. If the collateral's price falls, the coin can lose its peg [S3].
- **Commodity-backed** (Pax Gold): backed by gold. You must trust the issuer [S3].
- **Algorithmic:** no collateral. An algorithm grows or shrinks the supply to hold the price, and you must trust the algorithm [S3].
- Even Tether "can deviate from $1 under market stress" [S5].
- Uses: global transfers, trading pairs on exchanges, and predictable payments [S3].
- Size: the stablecoin market was about $235B as of March 2025 [S7]. ethereum.org's table lists USDT at about $175B (no as-of date) [S3].
- **(opinion)** "Stablecoins are crypto's first product-market-fit" [S7].

## Beginner analogy (AI-written, not from a source)
It's like a casino chip that is always worth exactly $1. You use it because it moves fast inside the system, not because you expect its value to change.
