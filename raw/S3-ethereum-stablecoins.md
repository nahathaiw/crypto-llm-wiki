## Notes
- Stablecoins are tokens designed to stay at a fixed value even when ETH's price changes; "cryptocurrencies without the volatility".
- Why: crypto prices swing, so spending it has an opportunity cost; stablecoins allow everyday payments. Example: 2010, two pizzas for 10,000 BTC (worth $41 then).
- Most are backed by reserve collateral; some use algorithms.
- Fiat-backed (USDC, USDT): bought/redeemed 1:1 with dollars; reserves back value. + stable; − centralized issuer, needs audits of reserves.
- Crypto-backed (DAI/USDS, USDe): overcollateralised (e.g. $1 coin backed by ≥$2 crypto); if collateral falls, more must be locked or the coin can lose value. + transparent, decentralized; − less stable.
- Precious-metal-backed (Pax Gold, Tether Gold): − centralized, must trust issuer.
- Algorithmic: no collateral; algorithm expands/shrinks supply to hold target price. − must trust the algorithm; balance changes.
- Uses: global transfers, trading pairs, predictable payments, lending for interest.
- Market table (no as-of date): USDT ≈ $174.6B, USDC ≈ $68.4B market cap. Time-sensitive.
