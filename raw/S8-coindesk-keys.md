## Notes
- A crypto wallet comes with two keys, public and private, that do complementary jobs.
- Public key: used to receive crypto; like a mailing address or account number that anyone can know. Sharing it does not let anyone withdraw your money.
- Private key: proves ownership. The wallet needs it to "sign" transactions that spend your funds, and the network can't validate a transaction without it.
- Private key = "a password to your crypto wallet", so keep it secret. Anyone who gets it can take all the crypto in that wallet.
- Private keys are numbers. Wallets often hide them behind a **seed phrase** ("secret recovery phrase"), which is a string of random words (example: MetaMask).
- Exchange wallets and custodians (e.g. Coinbase, Binance) hold the private key for you and control the funds on your behalf.
- Safety: write the key or seed phrase down and keep copies in several places. Don't screenshot or photograph it, because hackers target those copies.
- If the key is lost or stolen, there is "no way to recover it".
- ⚠ CLAIM FLAGGED AT INGEST: "The public key encrypts transactions, which can be decrypted only by the corresponding private key". This conflicts with S1, where transactions are *signed* with digital signatures, not encrypted. DO NOT USE.
- Not covered: how an address is derived, wallet types (hardware/software), seed phrase length, custody pros/cons.
